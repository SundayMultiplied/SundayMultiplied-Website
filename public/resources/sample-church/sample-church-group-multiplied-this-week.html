The Creator Who Is Still at Work

Sample Church • In the Beginning • Genesis 1:1–2 • This Week

Big Idea

The God who created everything is our sovereign Lord and living God, worthy of our worship and actively present in our redemption.

The Tension

We are constantly invited to define ourselves and decide what is right by personal happiness, cultural assumptions, or fear. At the same time, our sin makes us accountable to our Creator, and unfinished or painful circumstances can tempt us to wonder whether he is distant. Genesis confronts both drifts by showing us the God who has rightful authority over all he made and who remains attentively present with his creation.

Scripture Passage

Genesis 1:1–2

1 In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.

2 Now the earth was formless and void, and darkness was over the surface of the deep. And the Spirit of God was hovering over the surface of the waters.

Scripture quotations are from the Berean Standard Bible.

Sermon Snapshot

Genesis was first given to a people whose beliefs had been shaped by Egypt and who were preparing to enter a land filled with competing gods. Its opening words do not answer every modern question about how creation happened. They announce who created: the eternal God who made everything visible and invisible and who lacks nothing.

Because God created everything, he has authority over everything—including our identity, purpose, worship, and understanding of right and wrong. Yet Genesis 1:2 also shows that he is not a detached Creator. His Spirit hovers with attentive care, and the rest of Scripture shows this same God bringing gospel light into darkened hearts through Jesus. We can therefore repent of rival authorities, trust Christ rather than our own goodness, and rest in God’s nearness while he continues forming us.

Key Moments from the Message

  • Genesis was written to make clear the “who” of creation. Israel needed to know that the God who rescued them from Egypt was not one regional deity among many, but the eternal Creator of everything and everyone.
  • Acts 17 helped show that the Creator is not dependent on anything people can give him. God did not create because he was lonely or incomplete; creation flowed from the goodness and eternal fellowship of Father, Son, and Spirit.
  • Because people are made in God’s image, as Genesis 1:27 teaches, human dignity and identity are received from the Creator. Culture and personal happiness do not possess the authority to redefine what God has made.
  • The Spirit’s hovering in Genesis 1:2 was connected to the eagle fluttering over its young in Deuteronomy 32:10–12. The image shows a God who is attentive, protective, and near—not a Creator who made the world and walked away.
  • Second Corinthians 4:3–6 connects God’s command for light to shine in creation with the gospel light of Christ shining in human hearts. The God of creation is also the God of new creation, bringing forgiveness and life through Jesus.

Discussion Questions

Understand

  1. Why did the pastor say Genesis begins by answering “who” rather than every question about “how”?
  2. What do the words “created” and “hovering” reveal about both God’s authority and his relationship to creation?

Reflect

  1. Where do you feel the strongest pressure to let culture, preference, or happiness define what is true or good?
  2. When life feels unfinished or disordered, what do you naturally assume about God’s presence?

Apply

  1. What is one decision this week in which acknowledging God as Creator should change your response?
  2. How could you treat a person with whom you disagree in a way that visibly honors the image of God in them?

Practice the Message

The Situation

A Christian friend tells you, “I know what Scripture says, but this choice feels right to me and it makes me happy. I do not see why God should get to decide this part of my life.” Your friend expects either quick agreement or a harsh correction.

Your Task

Work together to prepare and rehearse a response that honors God as Creator while treating your friend as a person made in his image.

  1. Identify the competing source of authority in the statement and the deeper concern or desire underneath it.
  2. Choose one truth from the sermon that should guide your response: God’s authority as Creator, human dignity as his image-bearers, the accountability of sin, or God’s redeeming nearness in Christ.
  3. Write a two- or three-sentence response that combines truth, humility, and genuine care. Avoid slogans and include the words you would actually say.
  4. Have two people rehearse the conversation, then let the group revise the response so it remains faithful without becoming dismissive or combative.

Be Ready to Share

Share the response your group developed and explain how it reflects both the Creator’s authority and the dignity of the person hearing it.

Debrief

  1. How did seeing God as Creator shape what you affirmed, challenged, or invited your friend to consider?
  2. Did your first instinct lean toward avoiding truth, winning an argument, or protecting the relationship? What might that reveal about your heart?
  3. What helped your response communicate both conviction and the nearness of God’s grace in Christ?

Practice This Week

  • Before one meaningful decision, pause and ask, “What response recognizes that God is my Creator and Lord?”
  • Identify one person you tend to reduce to a disagreement or label, and choose a concrete way to honor that person’s God-given dignity.
  • When something feels unfinished, name it honestly in prayer and repeat: “God is near, attentive, and still at work.”

Midweek Reinforcement

Midweek check-in: Where have you needed to remember that God is both the Creator with authority and the living God who is near? Share one decision or unfinished place where that truth is changing your response.

Leader Tip

Questions about creation, identity, or morality can quickly become abstract or argumentative. Keep drawing the group back to the pastor’s central emphasis: who God is, what rightful authority belongs to him, and how his nearness in Christ changes the way we respond. During the practice activity, help participants use language that is both clear and genuinely caring.

Closing Prayer

Eternal Creator, you made us, sustain us, and know what is good. Forgive us for giving your authority to our preferences, fears, or culture. Teach us to worship you, honor every person made in your image, trust the saving work of Jesus, and rest in your nearness as you continue forming us. Amen.

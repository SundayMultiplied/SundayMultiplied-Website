Redefining Greatness

Southside Baptist Church · Week of 2026-08-09

Leader Orientation

This guide is designed for a 45–60 minute conversation. Use the questions that best serve your group; you do not need to finish everything.

Big Idea

Jesus redefines greatness by calling us away from pride and prominence toward childlike humility, serious resistance to sin, and Christlike care for people He values—all in dependence on Him.

The Tension

We are drawn to measure ourselves by success, recognition, comparison, and prominence. That pride can keep us from depending on God, make us excuse sin, influence others away from Him, and cause us to overlook people Jesus loves.

Scripture

Sermon Snapshot

The disciples' question about who was greatest revealed their competition and status assumptions. Jesus used a child to call them to humility, simple obedience, and faith—not childishness. The pastor did not condemn excellence or doing our best; he warned against letting success or being number one become the idol that drives us. Everyday humility depends on God, celebrates others, and measures faithfulness rather than prominence.

Jesus' severe language about stumbling, cutting off a hand or foot, and tearing out an eye communicates how seriously sin must be taken; the pastor identified it as hyperbole, not a literal instruction to maim the body. We are called to confess sin instead of excusing it, remove habits that nurture it, and examine whether our example is leading others toward God or away. We cannot overcome sin by ourselves; we must put faith in Jesus and depend on the Holy Spirit.

Jesus' picture of the one sheep shows God's concern for people who may be overlooked. The pastor did not claim certainty about whether the “little ones” are children, disciples, or both, and he emphasized that God's pursuit of one does not deny His care for the ninety-nine. Identity in God's family through Jesus frees us to value, pursue, and invest in people with genuine compassion—even when they have little to offer us. The sermon closes by calling us to repent, believe in Jesus, pursue holiness, and ask whether we have overlooked someone He deeply loves.

Key Moments

  • Jesus redirects the disciples from status and competition to childlike humility, obedience, faith, and dependence on God.
  • The pastor's practical contrast is to “encourage rather than compare” and ask “Was I faithful?” instead of “Did somebody notice me?”
  • Jesus' hyperbole calls us to take sin seriously, confess it, remove what nurtures it, and depend on Jesus and the Spirit rather than ourselves.
  • Our example matters because pride can lead us and people watching us away from God.
  • Christlike humility refuses to overlook people Jesus loves and invests in them with genuine compassion rather than asking what they can offer us.

Discuss · 22 minutes

Understand

  1. How do the pastor's three points—humble yourself, guard yourself, and value others the way Jesus would—work together to redefine greatness?
  2. What qualification did the pastor give about Jesus' language concerning the hand, foot, and eye?

Reflect

  1. Where does the desire for success, recognition, or being number one most affect your dependence on God or your response to other people?
  2. What sin, excuse, or habit do you need to take as seriously as Jesus does?

Apply

  1. Who might you be overlooking that Jesus deeply loves and values?
  2. What would faithfulness look like this week in the way your example affects someone watching you?

Practice the Message · 12 minutes

Situation

Someone else receives recognition you wanted, and a person with little influence or ability to help you is being overlooked. You feel comparison rising and know your response will be visible to others.

Task

Prepare and rehearse a response that depends on God, resists pride, encourages the person who succeeded, and values the person being overlooked.

  1. Name the comparison, pride, or desire for recognition that needs to be confessed rather than excused.
  2. Write the exact words you could use to encourage the person who received recognition.
  3. Choose one concrete way to notice or invest in the person being overlooked.
  4. Rehearse the response and identify how it could influence someone watching you toward God rather than away from Him.

Share

Share the words you rehearsed, the person you chose to notice, and the heart response you need Jesus and the Spirit to change.

Debrief

  • Which part of this response most challenged your usual definition of greatness?
  • How does depending on Jesus and the Spirit keep this from becoming self-powered behavior change?

Practice This Week

  • When comparison rises, encourage the other person and ask, “Was I faithful?”
  • Confess one sin you have been excusing and examine one habit that nurtures it, depending on Jesus and the Spirit for help.
  • Notice and invest in one person you might otherwise overlook, without measuring what that person can offer you.

Midweek Reinforcement

Midweek check-in: Was I faithful? What sin am I excusing, and whom might I be overlooking that Jesus deeply loves?

Leader Tip

Protect the pastor's qualifications: excellence is not inherently wrong, Jesus' severe language is hyperbole rather than a command to harm the body, and resisting sin depends on faith in Jesus and the Holy Spirit. If the conversation stays only with comparison, move the group into the sermon’s second and third movements.

Closing Prayer

Jesus, forgive our pride, comparison, and desire for prominence. Teach us childlike humility and faithfulness. Help us take sin seriously, confess what we excuse, and remove what draws us away from You. We cannot do this alone, so help us trust You and depend on Your Spirit. Open our eyes to people we overlook, and give us genuine compassion to value and invest in them as You do. Lead us in holiness, keep our example from leading others astray, and help us live as people who have repented and believed in You. Amen.
